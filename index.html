Notes on the method, and its honest limits

Spacetime

The exact Kerr metric in ingoing Kerr–Schild coordinates, which stay regular across both horizons, so you can fall through r+. For every pixel the null geodesic is integrated backwards from the camera with an adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) scheme on the Hamiltonian flow. A double-precision twin of the shader checks the GPU image to 0.01 px in the test suite. It also reproduces the 3√3 M shadow, Bardeen's Kerr shadow edges, the Carter-constant capture criterion and the weak-field deflection series.

Light

A thin Novikov–Thorne disk from the ISCO outward. Its temperature comes from the Page–Thorne flux for the chosen mass and Eddington ratio. Blackbody light shifted by g is again a blackbody, at temperature gT, so colours are exact: Planck spectra are integrated against the CIE 1931 observer in absolute photometric units. Stars are the 119,625 entries of the HYG catalogue, drawn as true point sources whose brightness follows the lensing magnification. The diffuse Milky Way comes from NASA's Deep Star Maps, with the stars removed.

The observer

Your camera is an orthonormal tetrad on a real timelike worldline: static, zero-angular-momentum, on a circular geodesic, or in free fall with optional rocket thrust. In Gyro mode the frame is Fermi–Walker transported, so you see geodetic and Lense–Thirring precession. The readouts give the full tidal tensor from the Riemann curvature, the proper acceleration needed to hold position, and the frequency shift of starlight.

Limits

The GPU works in 32-bit floats. The thin disk is optically thick with no color correction, limb darkening or returning radiation. Sky colours are shifted along the Planckian locus of each pixel's chromaticity. The turbulence texture and bloom are aesthetic. The sky gain is non-physical: the real Milky Way is about 1012 times fainter than the disk. Free fall stops at the inner (Cauchy) horizon, beyond which the Kerr solution is not predictive.
